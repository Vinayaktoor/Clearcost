How this works, and an honest note on the numbers

Since 2021, federal rule 45 CFR 180.50 has required every U.S. hospital to publish a machine-readable file of its standard charges: the gross price, the discounted cash price, and the rates it has negotiated with insurers. The intent was that patients could compare. The reality is that the files run to hundreds of thousands of rows, sit in different formats at every hospital, and are effectively unreadable. Closing that gap is the whole point of this tool.

These are real prices, parsed from the official filings that nine New York hospitals published in spring 2026: the Mount Sinai system (JSON files, around 130 to 150 MB each), NYU Langone (CSV files with 3,841 columns, around 470 MB each), and NewYork-Presbyterian (a 229 MB JSON covering several campuses). The parser streams each file, joins the same procedure across hospitals by its billing code, and reconciles the quirks real files throw at it.

Three things to keep in mind when reading the numbers:

  1. The default figure is the cash price, what someone paying cash is charged. For procedures where a hospital leaves that blank (many surgeries and deliveries), switch to the insurer rate tab.
  2. Hospitals in the same system share one price list, so every Mount Sinai campus shows the same cash price and every NYU campus shows the same cash price. The real gap is between systems, and that uniformity is itself a finding.
  3. The insurer range reflects what different plans pay; its high end can include outlier or case-rate contracts, so treat the range as indicative rather than a quote for your plan.

To refresh or extend it, drop new hospital files into the data folder and re-run the parser. Format and schema: the CMS reference on GitHub.

About these prices
